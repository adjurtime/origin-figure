# Default paper-figure style

Use this baseline only when the user, journal, or approved figure-suite specification does not provide a rule. Preserve justified project exceptions instead of forcing mechanical uniformity.

## Axes and scales

- Disable minor ticks by default. Retain them only when a log scale or a genuine fine-reading task needs them.
- Disable gridlines by default. Add a restrained major grid only when it materially improves comparison.
- Use short outward major ticks and a consistent, moderate axis-line weight.
- Aim for a compact set of readable major ticks rather than dense labels.
- Put units in axis titles and keep decimal places, percentages, scientific notation, and category order consistent.
- Preserve zero for magnitude-comparison bars. Explain or visibly signal a justified non-zero baseline.
- Give directly comparable panels the same range and tick interval. Do not force unrelated variables onto a common scale.
- Use either a left-and-bottom frame or a complete box consistently within the project; avoid duplicated ticks on all sides unless required.

## Typography and layout

- Use Times New Roman at about 10.5 pt at the final Word insertion size for English paper figures unless overridden.
- Keep a stable hierarchy for axis titles, tick labels, legends, direct labels, annotations, and panel letters.
- Align comparable panel rectangles, axes, baselines, margins, and gutters.
- Use a white background with deliberate whitespace. Avoid 3D effects, gradients, shadows, decorative borders, and other non-data ink.

## Colour, lines, and markers

- Use the smallest restrained, colourblind-aware palette that supports the scientific grouping.
- Keep the same scientific meaning in the same colour throughout an approved figure suite. Do not reuse one colour for conflicting meanings without an explicit exception.
- Pair colour with marker shape, line style, position, or direct labels when grayscale or accessibility matters.
- Keep line weights, marker sizes, marker borders, opacity, and category order consistent unless they encode data.

## Labels and legends

- Keep labels readable at final size and bound to worksheet columns. Resolve overlap through placement, abbreviation, or selective hierarchy without silently deleting important observations.
- Use black and grey label hierarchy only when it reflects legibility or emphasis consistently, not as arbitrary decoration.
- Match legend order to stack, curve, or semantic order. Remove technical filler series and unnecessary legend boxes.
- Keep panel labels, abbreviations, terminology, capitalization, and units consistent with the manuscript and caption.

## Exceptions

Record any exception that a reader could mistake for inconsistency, including different scales, retained grids, minor ticks, alternative colour mappings, or unusual label treatment. State the scientific or journal reason.
